Short answer: Yes, Survey Club is a real rewards app. It’s published on the US App Store by Your Finance Friend LLC, it pays for surveys, app offers and game milestones, and it cashes out to PayPal, Venmo, Cash App, bank transfer and gift cards. What makes it different is the paid membership: free members need $20 to withdraw, while Club Premium ($0.99/month) lowers the minimum to $2 and, according to the company, multiplies survey rewards. Whether it’s worth it comes down to one question: will you earn more than $0.99 extra a month?

This review is for US iPhone users. We looked at Survey Club’s App Store listing, its official website and privacy policy, recent App Store reviews, and an independent hands-on test published in November 2026.

What Survey Club is

Survey Club is a get-paid-to app with four main ways to earn, based on its store listing and website:

  • Paid surveys from research networks. Survey Club names partners including TapResearch, CPX Research, Pollfish, TheoremReach, BitLab and InBrain.
  • Cash offers: sign up for a service, install an app or try a product. The website says these go up to $125, though the biggest ones usually involve opening accounts or spending money.
  • Play-to-earn games with milestone rewards. The site lists titles such as Monopoly GO, Coin Master and Triple Tile.
  • Daily check-ins and location rewards, which the App Store description says pay you for “going about your day.”

Rewards show as dollar amounts, not points, which makes it easy to see what a task is worth.

One thing to know from an independent test: PaidFromSurveys found that many items labeled as surveys were really “App Survey” tasks, meaning you install an app, explore it, then answer a question. That’s closer to an app offer than a classic opinion survey.

The membership model: free vs Club Premium

Survey Club pitches itself as “subscription-based, not greed-based.” Its App Store description claims most apps keep about 70% of survey revenue while Survey Club takes 5%, so “if a survey pays $2.00, you keep $1.90.” We can’t verify those percentages; treat them as the company’s marketing.

Here’s what its website says each tier gets:

Free member Club Premium
Price $0 $0.99/month
Withdrawal minimum $20 $2
Survey rewards Standard Up to 2x to 3x (company claim)
Extras Cash-out bonuses up to $3, Club-only offer walls, priority support

The App Store also lists an annual membership ($9.99) and some $1.99 membership in-app purchases, so check the exact price on the screen before you subscribe.

Is Premium worth it? Only if you’ll actually use the app. A $0.99 subscription is cheap, but it’s still a cost that most big survey sites don’t charge. The honest way to decide: stay free for a week, see what you’d realistically earn, then subscribe only if Premium would clearly add more than $0.99 a month. If you do subscribe, you can cancel any time in your iPhone’s subscription settings.

Cash-out minimum and payout methods

Survey Club’s website currently lists a $20 minimum for free members and $2 for Club Premium. Older App Store screenshots show a $10 first withdrawal and $5 after that, and Reddit users have quoted other figures, so the threshold has clearly changed over time. The number on your Withdraw Cash screen is the one that counts.

Payout options listed by Survey Club:

  • PayPal
  • Venmo
  • Cash App
  • Bank transfer
  • Visa debit / prepaid card
  • Gift cards (the site says 400+ brands, including Amazon, Target and Apple)

Venmo and Cash App are the stand-outs. Many survey apps don’t offer them. Expect a phone verification step before your first cash-out; the PaidFromSurveys tester had to confirm a texted code.

How fast? The website promises “instant” payouts. Some App Store reviewers say money arrived within 12 to 24 hours; others describe cash-outs that were declined or delayed. Treat “instant” as best case.

Is Survey Club legit? What the evidence says

Points in its favor:

  • A named US developer (Your Finance Friend LLC) and a live App Store listing with frequent updates (version 1.34.0 in November 2026).
  • A clear privacy policy and in-app purchases disclosed on the store page.
  • Many App Store reviews describe successful PayPal and Venmo payouts.
  • An independent tester was able to sign up, earn and review the payout screens.

Reasons for caution:

  • It’s young. The app was first released in July 2025, so there’s no multi-year payout record.
  • Fraud flags and declined cash-outs. Some App Store reviewers say cash-outs were declined after several successful ones, with their account flagged.
  • Support. Several reviews mention tickets going unanswered for weeks, especially about missions that didn’t track.
  • Bold marketing. “Up to 3x more” and “highest-paying” are the company’s claims. The independent tester found free-member rewards average compared with bigger sites.

Our read: Survey Club pays real money and isn’t a scam, but its model nudges you toward a subscription, and the support complaints are worth taking seriously. Cash out as soon as you reach your minimum, and don’t let a balance build up.

How to use Survey Club safely

  1. Start free. Don’t subscribe on day one. Try surveys and one or two offers first.
  2. Use your real details. One account per person and device, no VPN. Mismatched details are a common reason accounts get flagged.
  3. Read every offer’s steps. Miss one and you may not get credited. Open games from inside the app so milestones track.
  4. Avoid paid offers you don’t want. Trials and purchase-based missions can cost more than they pay.
  5. Screenshot your progress before you contact support.
  6. Cash out at the minimum. Every time.

Privacy

Survey Club’s App Store privacy label says location and contact info may be used to track you across other companies’ apps and websites, and that contact details can be linked to you for third-party advertising. That’s common for rewards apps, which earn money from advertisers, but it’s worth knowing before you enable location rewards. You can turn off location access in iPhone Settings and still use surveys and offers.

The bottom line

Survey Club is a legitimate but unusual survey app: real payouts to PayPal, Venmo and Cash App, rewards shown in dollars, and a cheap subscription that unlocks its best features. Free members face a $20 minimum; Premium members get $2 for $0.99 a month. Use it as a secondary app, start free, cash out often, and only pay for Premium if your own numbers say it’s worth it.

Frequently asked questions

Is Survey Club legit?

Survey Club is a real app published on the US App Store by Your Finance Friend LLC, and many store reviewers say they have been paid to PayPal or Venmo. It is a newer app (first released in 2025) with a paid membership model, and some reviews report declined cash-outs, fraud flags or slow support, so cash out as soon as you can.

What is Survey Club's minimum payout?

Survey Club's website says the withdrawal minimum is $2 for Club Premium members and $20 for free members. Older App Store screenshots show lower thresholds, so check the Withdraw Cash screen in the app for the number that applies to your account.

How much does Survey Club Premium cost?

Club Premium costs $0.99 a month according to Survey Club's website, and the App Store lists monthly and annual membership in-app purchases. The company says Premium unlocks a 2x to 3x payout multiplier, the $2 minimum, cash-out bonuses and Club-only offer walls.

How does Survey Club pay you?

Survey Club says you can withdraw to PayPal, Venmo, Cash App, Visa debit and hundreds of gift card brands. Its app screenshots also show bank transfer as an option.

Is Survey Club the same as SurveyClub.com?

Be careful here. The app's website (getsurvey.club) points to surveyclub.com, but older SurveyClub help-desk articles describe a different model: panel invitations and Amazon gift cards from $25. If you read a help article, check that it describes the app you're using.

Does Survey Club work on Android?

The US App Store lists Survey Club as an iPhone app, and our partner link is for iPhone. If you're on Android, look at other survey apps instead.
