Short answer: PenguPace is legit, but it’s not really a “get paid to walk” app. It’s an Android rewards app from Scrambly where steps earn a tiny amount (roughly 4 cents per 10,000 steps in independent testing) and the meaningful money comes from sponsored game and app offers. Since 2026, step rewards also pause unless you complete at least one offer step every two days. The good news: you can cash out from $1, so it’s easy to check that it pays.

This review is written for US users. PenguPace is on Google Play; we didn’t find it on the US App Store.

What PenguPace is

PenguPace is a step-counting rewards app published by Scrambly, the company behind the Scrambly rewards app (Scrambly lists support at support@scrambly.io on the PenguPace listing). The Google Play description pitches it simply: walk, earn points, redeem them for gift cards or withdraw from $1.

In practice, PenguPace has two parts:

  1. Walking rewards. The app reads your phone’s step counter (or a connected step tracker). As you hit step milestones during the day, you claim coins in the app.
  2. Offers. Like Scrambly, PenguPace has sponsored games, apps and other offers that pay coins when you reach milestones.

The second part is where nearly all the earning potential is. That’s not a secret Scrambly hides, but it’s not what the “walk and earn” headline suggests either.

How much do steps actually pay?

Let’s do the math with sourced numbers:

  • On Scrambly’s apps, 1,000 coins = $1. (Scrambly’s minimum withdrawal is 1,000 coins, which it advertises as $1.)
  • An independent test by Fitcoin found 10,000 steps earned 40 coins, or about $0.04.
  • Scrambly’s help center says that since April 2026 its walking rewards can reach up to 160 coins per day at most, about $0.16.

So even a very active walker who hits the daily cap every single day would earn around $4.80 a month from steps (160 coins × 30 days). A typical day of normal walking earns a few cents. Steps are a small bonus, not an income.

That’s not a reason to avoid PenguPace. It’s a reason to download it with the right expectations.

The “1 offer step every 2 days” rule

This rule causes most of PenguPace’s one-star reviews, so it deserves its own section.

Scrambly’s developer replies on Google Play state: “Step rewards currently require completing 1 offer step every 2 days to stay active.” Scrambly’s help center explains the change from April 2026: it doubled the daily walking cap (from 80 to 160 coins) and, to keep the program sustainable, added the requirement to complete at least one offer step in any app or game offer every two days.

What that means for you:

  • An offer step means progressing a sponsored game or app offer to its next milestone, not just opening it.
  • If you go two days without an offer step, step rewards pause until you complete one.
  • Scrambly says support can suggest offers with quick milestones if you only want to keep walking rewards active.

If you hate the idea of installing sponsored games, PenguPace isn’t for you. If you don’t mind finishing a quick offer step now and then, the rule is manageable.

Cashing out from $1

This is PenguPace’s strongest point. The Google Play listing says you can withdraw from $1. Scrambly’s withdrawal rules, which the app follows, set the standard minimum at 1,000 coins, with some methods requiring more: bank transfer needs 10,000 coins (about $10).

Payout options reported for PenguPace include PayPal, a prepaid Visa and gift cards such as Amazon and Google Play. Exact options can vary, so check the withdrawal screen.

A $1 floor means you can test the whole process (verification, payout speed, delivery) with very little effort before deciding whether to keep going. Very few walking apps let you do that.

Verification: offer-app terms like Scrambly’s say identity verification may be required before a payout. In Scrambly’s main app, the first withdrawal requires photos of a US, UK or Canadian government ID plus a live selfie. Expect something similar.

Common complaints (and how to avoid them)

Reading recent reviews, the frustrations fall into a few buckets:

  1. “It stopped counting my steps.” Usually the two-day offer rule. Complete one offer step and step rewards resume.
  2. “A game milestone didn’t register.” Offer tracking can fail with any offerwall app. Install the game from inside PenguPace, don’t use a VPN, don’t reset your advertising ID, and screenshot milestones. If something doesn’t track, contact support well before the offer’s deadline.
  3. “Support is slow.” Some users report waiting a long time for replies on missing offer credit. Treat big game payouts as a nice surprise, not money you’ve already earned.

Privacy and permissions

PenguPace needs access to your step data (physical activity permission or a connected tracker), and its offers rely on tracking which apps you install. Check the Google Play data-safety section and Scrambly’s privacy policy before you start.

PenguPace or Scrambly?

They come from the same company and share the walking-reward approach. Choose PenguPace if you want a walking-first interface on Android. Choose Scrambly if you’re on iPhone, or if you mainly want game offers with walking as a side bonus. Running both doesn’t double your step earnings in any meaningful way, given how small step rewards are.

The bottom line

PenguPace is a legitimate app from an established rewards company, with an excellent $1 minimum. But walking alone earns cents, and step rewards require an offer step every two days. Download it if you’re happy to do the occasional sponsored game or app offer and want your steps to add a little on top. If you wanted to get paid purely for walking, no app on the US market pays meaningful money for that alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is PenguPace legit?

Yes, PenguPace is a real app published by Scrambly on Google Play, and it supports withdrawals from $1. The catch is that walking pays very little and step rewards require you to keep completing sponsored offers.

How much does PenguPace pay for 10,000 steps?

Independent testing reported 40 coins for 10,000 steps. With 1,000 coins equal to $1 on Scrambly's apps, that's about 4 cents. Scrambly says walking rewards can reach up to 160 coins (about 16 cents) per day at the maximum.

Why did PenguPace stop paying for my steps?

Scrambly's developer replies on Google Play say step rewards currently require completing at least one offer step every two days to stay active. If you skip that, step rewards pause until you complete an offer step.

What is the PenguPace minimum withdrawal?

The Google Play listing says you can withdraw from $1. Some methods, such as bank transfer, need a higher balance (Scrambly lists 10,000 coins, about $10, for bank transfer).

Is PenguPace available on iPhone?

We didn't find PenguPace on the US App Store in November 2026. iPhone users can get similar walking rewards in Scrambly's main app, which is on iOS.

Do I need ID to cash out from PenguPace?

Scrambly's terms say identity verification may be required before a payout. Scrambly's main app requires a one-time ID and selfie check before the first withdrawal.
