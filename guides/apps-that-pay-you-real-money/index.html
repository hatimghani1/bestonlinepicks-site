Survey apps pay for your opinion. How many surveys you qualify for depends on your profile, so fill it in honestly and completely.

3. Paid research studies

Research platforms connect you with universities and companies that pay for studies. On Prolific, researchers must pay at least $8 an hour, and you can cash out to PayPal once your approved balance reaches $6. New sign-ups may be waitlisted, and it works on a computer rather than a phone app. If you’re a student, campus studies are another option — see how to make money as a college student.

4. Gig apps (the most per hour)

Gig apps pay for real work — delivering food or groceries, driving, or doing local tasks — so they usually pay more for your time than rewards apps. Two things to know:

  • You need approval first, and often a car, bike or scooter allowed in your area.
  • Daily pay costs a fee. DoorDash Fast Pay lets US Dashers cash out once a day for $1.99; Uber Instant Pay costs $1.75 per cash-out to a personal debit card, up to 6 times a day.

Subtract gas, wear and taxes from what the app shows. The IRS says gig income is taxable even without a 1099.

5. Cash-back apps

Cash-back and receipt apps return a small percentage on purchases. They’re useful for groceries, gas or holiday gifts you were going to buy anyway — including Black Friday and Cyber Monday deals — but buying something just for the rebate costs you money. Swagbucks includes shopping cash back if you’d rather keep everything in one account.

How to pick the right app for you

  • Need money soon? Freecash (typically instant once approved) or Scrambly ($1 minimum). For cash today, combine an app with the ideas in how to make money in a day.
  • Love mobile games? Start with Freecash; add ZipRewards only if you can wait for its review.
  • Prefer surveys? Swagbucks or Survey Club on iPhone.
  • Have a car and a few hours? A gig app, once approved.
  • No money at all? Every app here is free — see how to make money with no money.

Use two apps at most at first, so you reach a cash-out minimum instead of spreading small balances across five apps.

Red flags: apps that don’t really pay

  • You must pay or deposit to join, level up or withdraw.
  • It promises a fixed daily income for tapping, liking or rating.
  • No company name, terms or privacy policy.
  • Withdrawals stay “pending” forever and recent reviews say the same.

Never pay to get paid. Report scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Frequently asked questions

What apps pay you real money?

Rewards apps (Freecash, Scrambly, Swagbucks), survey apps (Survey Club on iPhone), research platforms (Prolific), gig apps (DoorDash, Uber, Instacart, TaskRabbit) and cash-back apps all pay real money. Rewards and survey apps are the easiest to start on a phone; gig apps pay the most per hour but need approval and often a vehicle.

Which app pays real money instantly?

Freecash says PayPal and gift-card cash-outs are typically instant once approved, after you reach your first cash-out minimum and pass a one-time ID check. Scrambly lists $1 withdrawals that usually arrive within minutes after ID verification. 'Instant' rarely applies to your very first withdrawal because of those checks.

What is the best free app that pays you real money?

For most US adults we recommend Freecash first: it's free, works on iPhone, Android and the web, mixes games, surveys and tasks, and offers PayPal, Venmo, bank, crypto and gift-card cash-outs. Add Scrambly if you want a $1 minimum, or Swagbucks if you prefer surveys plus cash back.

How much can you make with apps that pay you?

Rewards and survey apps usually pay pocket money, and amounts depend on the offers available to you, your demographics for surveys, and how much time you put in. We don't publish earnings estimates we can't verify. Gig apps pay for hours of real work, minus fees and vehicle costs.

Are apps that pay you to play games real?

Yes, legitimate ones pay advertisers' rewards when you reach in-game milestones through their links. Read each offer's terms, skip any that need in-app purchases, and only play through the app's link so it tracks. Our guide to getting paid to play games explains how.

How do I spot a fake money app?

Fake apps ask you to pay to join, deposit money to 'unlock' a withdrawal, or promise a fixed daily income. Real apps show the reward per task, publish terms and a privacy policy, and never charge you to withdraw (beyond normal payment-method fees). Report scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
