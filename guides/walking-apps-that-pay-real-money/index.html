Short answer: Walking apps that pay real money exist in the US, but steps alone are worth cents a day. Using published numbers, 10,000 steps earns about 4 cents on Scrambly and PenguPace, and their walking rewards max out around 16 cents a day. That’s at most about $4.80 a month if you hit the cap every single day. Apps that pay more get it from sponsored offers (games, apps, sign-ups), not walking. Here’s how the main options compare, with the catches spelled out.

Where the numbers come from: Scrambly’s help center says that since April 2026 walking rewards can reach up to 160 coins per day, and its withdrawal minimum is 1,000 coins, which it advertises as $1. Independent testing of PenguPace (also made by Scrambly) found 10,000 steps = 40 coins.

So the realistic ceiling from steps alone is:

  • Average walker (~5,000-8,000 steps/day): a couple of cents a day, well under $1 a month.
  • Very active walker hitting the cap daily: about $0.16 × 30 = $4.80 a month.

That’s not nothing for steps you’d take anyway, but it’s not a side hustle.

Scrambly: best overall (iPhone & Android)

Scrambly is a rewards app that pays for game and app offers, with walking as a daily bonus. Why it leads this list:

  • $1 minimum to PayPal, Visa, Cash App and some gift cards, so you can actually cash out step-level amounts.
  • Fast payouts that Scrambly says arrive within minutes, after a one-time ID and selfie check.
  • Available on iPhone and Android, in the US, UK and Canada.

The catch: since April 2026 you must complete at least one offer step every 2 days to keep redeeming walking rewards. Scrambly made the change when it doubled the daily cap from 80 to 160 coins.

PenguPace: walking-first, Android only

PenguPace is Scrambly’s walking-focused Android app. It reads your phone’s step counter or a connected tracker, lets you claim coins at step milestones, and supports withdrawals from $1 (bank transfer needs more). It follows the same one offer step every 2 days rule; Scrambly’s developer replies on Google Play confirm it. We didn’t find PenguPace on the US App Store, so iPhone users should use Scrambly.

WeWard: cash payouts, no fixed rate

WeWard turns validated steps into Wards, which you can convert into a bank transfer, gift card, charity donation or other rewards on its Convert page. From WeWard’s help center:

  • No fixed exchange rate: the Wards needed vary by country, period and partner.
  • No transfers or gift cards in your first 14 days after signing up.
  • Transfers or gift cards usually arrive in 48 to 96 hours, 15 business days at most.
  • An ID is required for a bank transfer, and the account holder’s name must match.
  • You confirm each request through an emailed link within 60 minutes.

WeWard is a reasonable choice if you want a dedicated walking app with cash options, but because there’s no published rate, you’ll only know what your steps are worth by checking the Convert page in your country.

Sweatcoin: popular, but not cash

Sweatcoin is one of the best-known step apps, but its own help center says sweatcoins can’t be cashed out to PayPal or a bank account. You can spend them in its marketplace and enter prize draws. Cash only comes if you win a draw. If your goal is real money, Sweatcoin isn’t the right tool.

How to get the most from walking apps (without wasting time)

  1. Pick one app with a low minimum. Running five walking apps multiplies notifications, not money.
  2. Claim your steps. Several apps require you to tap to convert steps at milestones; unclaimed steps can be lost.
  3. Understand the offer rules. On Scrambly and PenguPace, plan a quick offer step every two days, or accept that step rewards will pause.
  4. Cash out regularly. With a $1 minimum there’s no reason to let a balance build.
  5. Never pay to “boost” steps. No legitimate step reward justifies a subscription or in-app purchase.
  6. Check permissions. These apps need activity data; some offers also track app usage.

Who walking apps are for

They’re for people who already walk and want a tiny reward on top, or who’ll do the occasional game or app offer anyway. They’re not for anyone hoping to earn meaningful money from walking alone. If you want bigger rewards, look at game offers in our game app reviews, where the same apps pay much more per task than they do per step.

The bottom line

Walking apps can pay real money in the US, but the real value of steps is a few cents a day. Scrambly is our top pick for its $1 minimum and fast payouts on iPhone and Android, with PenguPace as the Android walking-first alternative. WeWard pays cash with no fixed rate and a 14-day wait, and Sweatcoin doesn’t cash out to PayPal at all. Walk for your health first, and treat the rewards as a small bonus.

Frequently asked questions

Which walking app pays the most real money?

No walking app pays much for steps alone. Among apps with published numbers, Scrambly's walking rewards top out at 160 coins (about 16 cents) per day. Apps that pay more do it through sponsored game and app offers, not steps.

How much is 10,000 steps worth?

On Scrambly and PenguPace, independent testing found about 40 coins, roughly 4 cents. WeWard has no fixed exchange rate between Wards and money; it varies by country and period. Sweatcoin doesn't publish a cash value and can't be cashed out to PayPal.

Can you really get paid to walk?

Yes, but only small amounts. Plan on cents per day from steps. Some apps add requirements, such as completing an offer step every two days on Scrambly and PenguPace.

Which walking apps pay to PayPal?

Scrambly and PenguPace offer PayPal withdrawals from $1. WeWard offers transfers including PayPal or Venmo, but not in your first 14 days. Sweatcoin says sweatcoins can't be cashed out to PayPal or a bank account.

Are walking apps safe?

Mainstream ones are legitimate, but they collect activity data and often ask for ID before cash-outs. Read the privacy policy, grant only the permissions you need, and never pay for an 'upgrade' that promises to multiply step earnings.
